# Question

Diagrams A, B, and C below show three situations in which one or two forces act on a puck that moves over frictionless ice along an x axis, in one-dimensional motion. The puck’s mass is $m = 0.20 kg$.
The forces $\overrightarrow{F_1} of magnitude 4.0 N$ and $\overrightarrow{F_2} of magnitude of 2.0 N$ are directed along the x axis.
And force $\overrightarrow{F_3}$ of magnitude 1.0 N is directed at angle $\theta = 30 \degree$.
Calculate in each situation, A,B and C the acceleration of the puck.

## A

![](/img/puck_diagram_a.png)

The only force on the system is $\overrightarrow{F_1}$ therefore ${F_{net}} = 4 N$.
Applying Newton's second law of motion to the situation:
$$
4 = 0.20 \cdot a \Rightarrow a = \frac{4}{0.20} = 20 \frac{m}{s^2}
$$

## B

![](/img/puck_diagram_b.png)

Here the situation is a bit different as a secondary force has been introduced on the system, $\overrightarrow{F_2}$:

$$
F_{net} = {F_1} + {F_2} = 4 + (-2) = 2 N
$$

$$
\Rightarrow 2 = 0.2 \cdot a \Rightarrow a = \frac{2}{0.2} = 10 \frac{m}{s^2}
$$

## C

![](/img/puck_diagram_c.png)


In this occassion $\overrightarrow{F_2}$ and $\overrightarrow{F_3}$ are the only vectors who inflict force on the system. and $\overrightarrow{F_3}$ seems to be influencing both the $x$ and $y$ axes.


If we resolve $F_3$ to it's components we receive:
$$
F_{3,x} = 1 \cdot \cos 30 = 0.866 N, F_{3,y} = 1 \cdot \sin 30 = 0.5
$$

$$
F_{net,x} = F_{2,x} + F_{3,x} = -2 + 0.86 = - 1.14 N, F_{net,y} = F_{2,y} + F_{3,y} = 0 + 0.5 = 0.5 N 
$$

$$
\vec{a_{x}} = \frac{-1.14}{0.2} = -5.7 \frac{m}{s^2}, \vec{a_{y}} = \frac{0.5}{0.2} = 2.5 \frac{m}{s^2}
$$ 

Because we the motion only occures in one dimension (the x axis) we can neglect $a_y$ and conclude:
$$
a_x = -5.7 \frac{m}{s^2}
$$
