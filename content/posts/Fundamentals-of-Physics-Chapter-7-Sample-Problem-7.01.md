# Question
In 1896 in Waco, Texas, William Crush parked two locomotives at opposite ends of a 6.4-km-long track, fired them up, tied their throttles open, and then allowed them to crash head-on at full speed.
Assuming each locomotive weighed $1.2 X 10^6\ N$ and its acceleration was a constant $0.26\   m/s2$, what was the total kinetic energy of the two locomotives just before the collision?

# Answer
As we know that each locomotive is traveling at a constact acceleration I can use the formula for:
$$
v^2 = {v_0}^2 + 2 \cdot a (x - x_0)
$$

$$
\Rightarrow  v_2 = 0^2 + 2 \cdot 0.26 \cdot (\frac{6.4}{2}\cdot 10^3) = 1664 \Rightarrow v = \sqrt{1664} = 40.79\ m/s
$$

To find the kinetic energy on impact I can use the kinetic energy formula
$$
K = \frac{1}{2} \cdot m \cdot {v}^2
$$

The mass of both trains can be derived from the their total weight:

$$
m = \frac{w}{g} = \frac{2 \cdot 1.2 X 10^6}{9.8} = 244897.95\ kg
$$

$$
\Rightarrow K = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 244897.95 \cdot 40.79^2 = 203733555.625\ J
$$
