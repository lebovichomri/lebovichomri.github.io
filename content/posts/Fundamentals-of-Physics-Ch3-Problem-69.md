# Questions

A wheel with a radius of 45.0 cm rolls without slipping along a horizontal floor:
![](/img/wheel_rolls.png)

At time $t1$, the dot $P$ painted on the rim of the wheel is at the point of contact between the wheel and the floor. At a later time $t2$, the wheel has rolled through one-half of a revolution.
What are (a) the magnitude and (b) the angle (relative to the floor) of the displacement of $P$?

# Answers

(a) As we know that from $t1$ to $t2$ the wheel has rolled exactly through one-half of a revolution, in other words has rolled through exactly $\frac{1}{2}$ of it's circumference.
We can calculate the distance traveled by the wheel via:
$$
\frac{2 \pi r}{2} = 45 \pi
$$

![](/img/wheel_rolls_0.png)

We then get two vectors, let's call them $\vec{r_1}$, of magnitude $45\pi cm$, and $\vec{r_2}$, of $90 cm$.

let $\vec{r3} = \vec{r_1} + \vec{r_2}$, we can calculate the $\vec{r_3}$'s magnitude, via pythagoras:
$$
r_3 = \sqrt{r_1^2 + r_2^2} \Rightarrow r_3 = 167.588\ cm
$$

(b) The desired angle to calculate is defined as $\phi$ in the diagram:

![](/img/wheel_rolls_1.png)

which can be easily calculated using trigonometry:
$$
\tan \phi = \frac{90}{45 \pi} \Rightarrow \phi = 32.48163659 \degree
$$
